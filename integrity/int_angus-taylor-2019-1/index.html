Integrity matter

Police assessment of altered City of Sydney travel figures cited in Mr Taylor's 2019 letter to the Lord Mayor of Sydney; no further investigation by the Australian Federal Police

Angus Taylor · Liberal · 25 October 2019

No finding

Outcome: No finding. The matter concluded without any finding against the person.

Description

In September 2019 Mr Taylor, then Minister for Energy and Emissions Reduction, wrote to the Lord Mayor of Sydney citing City of Sydney travel spending figures that did not match the council's published annual report; the figures were reported by The Daily Telegraph. After the Shadow Attorney-General referred the matter to NSW Police, Strike Force Garrad investigated and in December 2019 referred the matter to the Australian Federal Police because the alleged conduct occurred in Canberra. On 6 February 2020 the AFP announced it would not investigate further, stating there was no evidence to indicate the Minister was involved in falsifying information and citing the Minister's apology to the Lord Mayor, the low level of harm and the resources that further investigation would require. The Commonwealth Ombudsman later examined the AFP's handling and found its decision lawful, while noting that the AFP had not tested its resource concerns by seeking further information from the Minister's office. Mr Taylor denied that he or his staff altered the document and apologised for relying on incorrect figures.

Status history

  1. Allegation25 October 2019

    The Shadow Attorney-General wrote to the NSW Police Commissioner alleging that figures in Mr Taylor's letter of 29 September 2019 to the Lord Mayor were drawn from an altered version of the City of Sydney's annual report. The same day Mr Taylor tabled a statement saying two versions of the report had appeared on the council's website.

  2. Formal investigation26 October 2019

    NSW Police confirmed that Strike Force Garrad was investigating (date per the Commonwealth Ombudsman's chronology). On 31 October 2019 Mr Taylor wrote to the Lord Mayor acknowledging the figures were incorrect and apologising; on 25 November 2019 he told the House that neither he nor his staff had altered the document.

  3. Referral17 December 2019

    NSW Police referred the matter to the Australian Federal Police, as the alleged conduct occurred in Canberra, and finalised its own investigation. The AFP confirmed receipt of the referral on 2 January 2020.

  4. No finding6 February 2020

    The AFP announced it would not investigate further: there was 'no evidence to indicate the Minister for Energy and Emissions Reduction was involved in falsifying information' and further investigation was unlikely to obtain sufficient evidence to substantiate a Commonwealth offence. No charges were laid.

  5. No finding16 November 2020current

    The Commonwealth Ombudsman reported on the AFP's handling of the referral, finding the AFP's decision was 'lawful and open to it' while observing that the AFP had not tested its resource concerns by seeking further information from the Minister's office by less formal means. The report made no finding about Mr Taylor.

Outcome

No charges. Australian Federal Police, 6 February 2020: 'The AFP assessment of this matter identified there is no evidence to indicate the Minister for Energy and Emissions Reduction was involved in falsifying information ... the AFP has determined it is unlikely further investigation will result in obtaining sufficient evidence to substantiate a Commonwealth offence. The AFP now considers this matter finalised.' Commonwealth Ombudsman, 16 November 2020: it was 'lawful and open to it to refrain from further investigating the matter'.

Politician’s response

2 January 2020 · denial

Mr Taylor denied that he or any member of his staff altered the document, apologised to the Lord Mayor for relying on incorrect figures, and said he would cooperate with any investigation. After the AFP's decision he criticised the referral as a political tactic.

“I reject absolutely the suggestion that I, or any members of my staff, altered the documents in question.”
