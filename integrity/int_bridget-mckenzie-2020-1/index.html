Integrity matter

Auditor-General's audit of the Community Sport Infrastructure Program and the finding by the Secretary of the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet that Senator McKenzie breached the Statement of Ministerial Standards

Bridget McKenzie · Nationals · 15 January 2020

Official finding

Description

On 15 January 2020 the Auditor-General reported on the $100 million Community Sport Infrastructure Program, administered by Sport Australia with Senator McKenzie, as Minister for Sport, approving the grants. The report found that funding decisions departed from Sport Australia's merit assessments, that the approach documented in the minister's office focused on marginal and targeted electorates, and that there was no evident legal authority for the minister's approval role. The Prime Minister asked the Secretary of the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet to advise whether the Statement of Ministerial Standards had been breached. The Secretary advised that she had acted within the program guidelines and had not breached the Standards with respect to fairness, but that she had breached them by failing to declare her membership of two organisations and had an actual, undeclared and unmanaged conflict of interest in awarding $35,980 to the Wangaratta Clay Target Club, of which she was a member. She resigned from the ministry and as Deputy Leader of the Nationals on 2 February 2020 and remained in the Senate.

Official finding

Status history

  1. Official finding15 January 2020

    Auditor-General's report presented to Parliament: the award of grant funding was not informed by an appropriate assessment process and sound advice, successful applications were not those assessed as most meritorious, there was evidence of distribution bias toward marginal and targeted electorates, and no evident legal authority for the minister to approve the grants.

  2. Formal investigation17 January 2020

    The Prime Minister sought advice from the Secretary of the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet under paragraph 7.4 of the Statement of Ministerial Standards on any apparent breaches in relation to the minister's administration of the program.

  3. Official finding2 February 2020current

    The Prime Minister announced the Secretary's conclusion that Senator McKenzie had breached the Standards by failing to declare memberships of two organisations and had an actual, undeclared and unmanaged conflict of interest in awarding a grant to the Wangaratta Clay Target Club, while finding no breach with respect to fairness. She resigned from the ministry and as Deputy Leader of the Nationals the same day.

Outcome

Secretary of the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet, as stated in his submission to the Senate Select Committee: 'I concluded Senator McKenzie did not act in breach of the Standards with respect to fairness. I did find that Senator McKenzie breached the Standards by failing to declare her memberships of two organisations and that she had an actual conflict of interest when awarding funding to one of those organisations, the Wangaratta Clay Target Club, which was neither declared to the Prime Minister nor managed.' Senator McKenzie resigned from the ministry on 2 February 2020. The Secretary's full report has not been published.

Politician’s response

2 February 2020 · statement

After the audit report Senator McKenzie said every funded project was eligible and no rules were broken. On 2 February 2020 she said she accepted the Secretary's report, denied that her club memberships had influenced her decisions or that she received any personal gain, and acknowledged that failing to declare the memberships in a timely manner breached the Standards.

“I maintain that at no time did my membership of shooting sports clubs influence my decision making, nor did I receive any personal gain. However, I acknowledge that my failure to declare my memberships in a timely manner constituted a breach of the Prime Minister's ministerial standards.”
