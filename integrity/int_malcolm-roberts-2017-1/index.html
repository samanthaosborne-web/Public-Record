Integrity matter

High Court ruling that the 2016 election was void under section 44(i) of the Constitution

Malcolm Roberts · One Nation · 9 August 2017

Official finding

Description

On 9 August 2017 the Senate referred questions about Senator Roberts's eligibility to the Court of Disputed Returns after reports that he held British citizenship by descent when he nominated for the 2016 election. On 22 September 2017 Justice Keane found that Senator Roberts was a British citizen at nomination, knew there was at least a real and substantial prospect that he was, and had not taken effective steps to renounce it before nominating; his renunciation took effect on 5 December 2016. On 27 October 2017 the Full Court unanimously held that he had been ineligible to be elected and ordered a special count, under which Fraser Anning was declared elected. In June 2019 the High Court ordered Senator Roberts to pay $6,000 plus costs in an action under the Common Informers (Parliamentary Disqualifications) Act 1975 for the sitting days on which he sat while ineligible. He was elected to the Senate again in 2019.

Politician’s response

16 July 2019 · statement

Before the referral Senator Roberts said he had always considered himself Australian. After the 2019 order he said he had complied with the Constitution immediately and that the parties had waited months for the court to formalise an agreed outcome.

“I immediately complied with the Constitution's provisions. We then waited many, many months for the High Court to seal the agreement, as it did recently.”
