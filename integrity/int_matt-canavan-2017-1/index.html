Integrity matter

High Court ruling that Senator Canavan was not disqualified under section 44(i) of the Constitution (Re Canavan)

Matt Canavan · Nationals · 25 July 2017

Cleared

Outcome: Cleared. The responsible body found no wrongdoing.

Description

In July 2017 Senator Canavan disclosed that he might hold Italian citizenship by descent, registered by his mother in 2006 without his knowledge, and stood aside from the ministry while the question was tested. The Senate referred the question of whether he was incapable of being chosen under section 44(i) to the High Court sitting as the Court of Disputed Returns, alongside references concerning six other parliamentarians. On 27 October 2017 the court held that, on the evidence of Italian law before it, one could not be satisfied that he had ever been an Italian citizen, and answered that there was no vacancy in his Senate place. He returned to the ministry the same day.

Status history

  1. Allegation25 July 2017

    Senator Canavan announced that his mother had registered him as an Italian citizen in 2006 without his knowledge and stood aside from the ministry; the Attorney-General said the matter would be referred to the High Court.

  2. Referral9 August 2017

    The Senate referred questions concerning his qualification to the Court of Disputed Returns (the judgment records the President of the Senate's letter of 9 August 2017). His renunciation of any Italian citizenship took effect on 8 August 2017.

  3. Cleared27 October 2017current

    The Full Court unanimously answered that there was no vacancy by reason of section 44(i) in the Queensland Senate place for which he was returned. He resumed his cabinet post the same day.

Outcome

Order of the Court of Disputed Returns: 'There is no vacancy by reason of s 44(i) of the Constitution in the representation of Queensland in the Senate for the place for which Senator the Hon Matthew Canavan was returned.' Reasons at [86]: 'On the evidence before the Court, one cannot be satisfied that Senator Canavan was a citizen of Italy.'

Politician’s response

25 July 2017 · denial

Senator Canavan said he had not known of, or requested, Italian citizenship, stood aside from cabinet pending the court's decision, and later formally renounced any Italian citizenship.

“I had no knowledge that I had become an Italian citizen, nor had I requested to become an Italian citizen.”
