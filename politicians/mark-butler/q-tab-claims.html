“Today, Shadow Health Minister Anne Ruston confirmed Australians will not get free GP visits under Peter Dutton and the Liberals”

26 February 2025 · Media release by the Minister for Health and Aged Care, following the Shadow Health Minister's appearance at Senate estimates

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryMedia release· 26 February 2025
    Media release: Shadow Health Minister confirms Australians will pay to see a GP under Peter Duttonmarkbutler.net.au

    Office of Mark Butler

Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

The Senate estimates recording of 26 February 2025 shows Senator Ruston was arguing that Labor's social media posts about its $8.5 billion plan to lift bulk billing to 90 per cent by 2030 were misleading because not every visit would be free. She said the Coalition had 'never said that Australians will get access to free GP services without exception', then confirmed the Coalition supported bulk billing and, asked whether she wanted Australians to have free access, replied 'No, I do want them to have free access'. The Coalition had committed to match the bulk-billing policy. Other quotes in the release, from Sussan Ley and Peter Dutton, concerned free TAFE and energy and university-debt subsidies rather than Medicare.

Original statement

“had been in freefall after a decade of cuts and neglect to Medicare by the former government”

29 April 2024 · Media release, 'Bulk billing slide stopped thanks to Albanese Government', repeated the same day in interviews on ABC News Breakfast, ABC Radio AM and Sky News

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryMedia release· 29 April 2024
    Media release: Bulk billing slide stopped thanks to Albanese Government, 29 April 2024health.gov.au

    Minister for Health and Aged Care

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

Department of Health Medicare statistics for GP non-referred attendances, the measure used in the minister's own release, show the bulk-billing rate rose from 83.1 per cent in the December quarter 2013 to 87 per cent in the June quarter 2022, peaking at 92.5 per cent in June 2020 during pandemic telehealth arrangements. The rate was declining from mid-2021 but fell much faster after the May 2022 election, to 76.5 per cent by the end of 2023; even after the two-point rise the minister cited following the November 2023 incentive increase, the March quarter 2024 rate was about ten points below its level at the election. Health economists told AAP FactCheck the rate was 'certainly declining' before the election but not in free fall, and that the decline was largely structural and would have occurred under either government.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    Medicare statistics collectionhealth.gov.au

    Department of Health and Aged Care — Quarterly GP bulk-billing rates: 83.1 per cent (December quarter 2013), 92.5 per cent peak (June quarter 2020), 87 per cent (June quarter 2022), 76.5 per cent (end of 2023).

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report
    Increases to bulk billing incentive paymentshealth.gov.au

    Department of Health and Aged Care — The November 2023 tripling of the bulk-billing incentive credited for the subsequent rise.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryTranscript
    Television interview with Minister Butler, ABC News Breakfast, 29 April 2024health.gov.au

    Minister for Health and Aged Care — Repetition of the 'free fall' description the same day.

Independent evidence

  • Tier 2 · IndependentFact check· 30 May 2024
    Health minister's bulk billing 'free fall' claim misleadsaap.com.au

    AAP FactCheck — Verdict as published: Misleading.

Contradictory evidence

The primary evidence listed above supplies the context the claim needs; no separate contradicting source is attached.

Context

Mr Butler's office confirmed the release's figures referred to GP non-referred attendances but, as the basis for 'free fall', pointed to a different measure, the share of adult concession card holders always bulk billed, which it said fell five percentage points in the four years to June 2022; it declined to provide comparable post-election figures for that measure. AAP FactCheck's 2025 election coverage recorded the Prime Minister using the 'free fall' description and referred back to this verdict.

Politician response

  • 30 May 2024 · defence

    Mr Butler's office confirmed the release's figures referred to GP non-referred attendances, pointed to the share of adult concession card holders always bulk billed as the basis for 'free fall', and declined to provide comparable post-election figures for that measure.
