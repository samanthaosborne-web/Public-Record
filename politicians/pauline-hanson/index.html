Parliamentary career

Elected to the House of Representatives for Oxley, Queensland, at the 1996 general election and defeated at the 1998 election; the Parliament of Australia records her party service in that period as Independent from 2 March 1996 to 27 June 1997 and as Pauline Hanson's One Nation from 27 June 1997 to 3 October 1998. Elected to the Senate for Queensland at the 2016 election and re-elected in 2022. Recorded as Leader of the Federal Parliamentary Pauline Hanson's One Nation from 2 July 2016 and as Leader of One Nation from 28 January 2026, when the party's name changed in the parliamentary record.

28 July 2026 · Video posted on Instagram, in which she said 739,000 people were brought in between 2022 and 2023 and only 51,605 were skilled

Original source

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

The figures come from three datasets that do not measure the same thing. ABS data record about 737,000 overseas arrivals in 2022–23, including returning Australians, of whom 83,700 held skilled visas. The 51,605 figure is the number of temporary skilled visas granted to primary applicants that year, not arrivals, and a further 68,922 permanent skilled visas were granted. The Department of Home Affairs told AAP FactCheck that 10,589 temporary and permanent visas were granted to construction-industry occupations in 2022–23; the 1,800 figure is close to the 1,626 permanent and 1,967 temporary visas granted on a narrower list of core construction trades.

Original statement

“Labor has been bringing more than 1375 people per day for the past three years”

17 June 2026 · Speech, National Press Club, Canberra

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryTranscript· 17 June 2026
    National Press Club address, 17 June 2026 (video, from 10:18)youtu.be

    National Press Club of Australia (YouTube)

Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

ABS net overseas migration data to September 2025 gave 1.23 million net migrants over the preceding three years, an average of about 1,122 a day. The 1,375 figure corresponds to total population growth per day in an analysis published by The Australian, which includes natural increase (births minus deaths); that analysis put daily net migration at 1,091. Later ABS data to December 2025 put the three-year average at about 1,060 a day.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    National, state and territory population, September 2025abs.gov.au

    Australian Bureau of Statistics — Net overseas migration data available when the statement was made: about 1,122 a day over three years.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    National, state and territory population, December 2025abs.gov.au

    Australian Bureau of Statistics — Net overseas migration averaged 825 a day in 2025 and about 1,060 a day over three years.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report
    Natural increasepopulation.gov.au

    Centre for Population, Australian Government — Natural increase is births minus deaths, a component of population growth distinct from migration.

Original statement

“around 130,000 Australians are sleeping rough every night”

17 June 2026 · Speech, National Press Club, Canberra

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryTranscript· 17 June 2026
    National Press Club address, 17 June 2026 (video, from 14:22)youtu.be

    National Press Club of Australia (YouTube)

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

The ABS 2021 Census estimated 122,494 people were homeless on census night, a figure close to the one used. Of those, 7,636 (6 per cent) were in the 'improvised dwellings, tents or sleeping out' category the ABS treats as sleeping rough; the largest group, 47,895 (39 per cent), were living in severely crowded dwellings. The number is approximately right for homelessness under the ABS definition and about 17 times the ABS figure for people sleeping rough.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    Estimating Homelessness: Census, 2021abs.gov.au

    Australian Bureau of Statistics — 122,494 people homeless on census night 2021; 7,636 in improvised dwellings, tents or sleeping out; 47,895 in severely crowded dwellings.

Contradictory evidence

The primary evidence listed above supplies the context the claim needs; no separate contradicting source is attached.

Context

The ABS definition of homelessness is broader than 'rooflessness' and includes severely crowded dwellings, supported accommodation, boarding houses and staying temporarily with other households.
