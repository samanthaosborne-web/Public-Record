Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

NAB's 17 August 2026 economic newsletter described an illustrative scenario: if rental yields rose by one percentage point and property prices stayed flat, Sydney and Melbourne rents would need to rise by 25 to 30 per cent. Mr Taylor's office said the figure compared the ABS net overseas migration estimate for the year to September 2025 (311,000) with the forecast of 225,000 for 2026-27 in the December 2024 Mid-Year Economic and Fiscal Outlook, a gap of 86,000. Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryTranscript· 19 March 2026

    Angus Taylor (Facebook)

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

A Parliamentary Library chronology shows that the Morrison government's Fuel Quality Standards (Petrol) Determination 2019 set a 10 parts-per-million sulphur limit from 1 July 2027, and that in 2021-22 the Coalition paid Ampol and Viva Energy $137.5 million each to meet the lower limit by the end of 2024 without legislating that date. The Albanese government legislated a 2024 deadline in 2022, then moved it to December 2025. Ampol told the ASX in October 2025 that its refinery upgrade would not be finished before mid-2026, and it exported higher-sulphur petrol in the meantime. After the standards were temporarily relaxed from 12 March 2026, Ampol said it could supply an extra 80 to 100 million litres a month domestically. The exports were connected to the compliance deadline the current government set, but the standard itself originated under the Coalition.

“In the first 18 months of Labor's time in office, personal income tax had increased by a record 27 per cent”

15 January 2024 · Opinion piece in The Australian

Original source

  • Tier 2 · IndependentNews report· 15 January 2024
    Opinion piece: 'Australians deserve unambiguous commitment to stage three tax cuts'theaustralian.com.au

    The Australian (opinion piece by Angus Taylor)

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

ABS national accounts show income tax paid by individuals rose 27.3 per cent in nominal terms between the March quarter 2022 ($71.5 billion) and the September quarter 2023 ($91.0 billion). That period includes about two months of the previous Coalition government; from the June quarter 2022 the rise was 23.9 per cent. The rise was not a record: larger 18-month increases occurred in 1963-65 (43.5 per cent), 1969-70 (40.9 per cent), 1973-74 (107.2 per cent) and in the first 18 months of the Fraser government (41.7 per cent). Measured as a share of gross household income, income tax rose from 15.1 to 17.2 per cent, an increase of 13.6 per cent, which was also not a record.
