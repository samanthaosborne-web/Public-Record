Parliamentary career

Elected to the House of Representatives for Grayndler, New South Wales, at the 1996 election and re-elected at every election since (1998, 2001, 2004, 2007, 2010, 2013, 2016, 2019, 2022 and 2025). The Parliament of Australia records his Australian Labor Party service from 2 March 1996. He has no Senate or state parliamentary service.

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryHansard· 6 November 2025
    House of Representatives Hansard, 6 November 2025parlinfo.aph.gov.au

    Parliament of Australia

Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

Funding data supplied to AAP FactCheck by Foodbank Australia showed its total federal funding fell from $1.4 million in 2013-14 to $1.3 million in 2014-15, $1.05 million in 2015-16 and $750,000 a year from 2016-17 to 2018-19, before rising to $16.2 million in 2019-20 during the pandemic. That was a reduction, but far smaller than $20 million a year. Department of Social Services financial statements show that spending on the broader financial wellbeing and capability program, which funds Foodbank among other hardship charities, fell by about $25 million a year between 2014-15 ($124.94 million) and 2017-18 ($99.97 million), which AAP FactCheck said supported the Treasurer's broader statement about 'these programs' but not the Foodbank-specific figure.

3 November 2025 · House of Representatives, Question Time, answering an opposition question on cost of living and food security

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryHansard· 3 November 2025
    House of Representatives Hansard, 3 November 2025aph.gov.au

    Parliament of Australia

Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

Voting records and shadow-minister statements show the Coalition supported several of the government's measures: it matched the $8.5 billion Medicare bulk-billing commitment dollar for dollar in February 2025, spoke and voted for the legislation cutting the PBS co-payment in September 2025, supported the $573 million women's health package, announced a matching freeze on draught beer excise in March 2025, and supported the 2024 extension of paid parental leave to 26 weeks while proposing amendments. The Coalition did oppose other measures, including the student debt cut, free TAFE, the Housing Australia Future Fund and build-to-rent tax changes.

Original statement

“One of the things that my government has done, of course, is introduce energy bill relief - three lots of energy bill relief, all of which were opposed by those opposite”

31 July 2025 · House of Representatives, Question Time, responding to a question from the Leader of the Opposition

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryHansard· 31 July 2025
    House of Representatives Hansard, 31 July 2025aph.gov.au

    Parliament of Australia

Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

Parliamentary records show the Coalition voted against the December 2022 legislation that created the Energy Bill Relief Fund and funded the first round, although the then Opposition Leader said it would have supported the subsidies if they had been separated from the coal and gas price caps in the same bill. The $300 extension in the 2024-25 budget passed in an appropriation bill without Coalition opposition after the Opposition Leader confirmed support in his budget reply of 17 May 2024. The third round, announced in the 2025-26 budget, lapsed at the dissolution of Parliament, was reintroduced in July 2025, and the then Shadow Treasurer had said in March 2025 the Coalition would not oppose it. AAP FactCheck found no evidence the Coalition opposed the second or third rounds.
