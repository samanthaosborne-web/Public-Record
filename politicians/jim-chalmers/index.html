“Angus Taylor is the architect of the Liberal Party's policy for higher taxes on workers, bigger deficits and more debt”

16 February 2026 · Interview, Today program, Channel Nine

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryTranscript· 16 February 2026
    Transcript: interview with Sarah Abo, Today, Channel 9ministers.treasury.gov.au

    Treasurer of Australia

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

The Parliamentary Budget Office's 2025 Election Commitments Report found that the Coalition's policies would have increased deficits in 2025-26 and 2026-27 but reduced cumulative deficits by $6.6 billion over the four years to 2028-29, with net debt $49.9 billion and gross debt $81.7 billion lower over that period. Over the medium term, deficits were projected to be $162.4 billion larger between 2031-32 and 2035-36 and net debt $275.6 billion higher between 2033-34 and 2035-36, driven mainly by the Coalition's commitment to defence spending of 3 per cent of GDP and its 23.9 per cent tax-to-GDP cap. Budget experts consulted by AAP FactCheck said the claim was reasonable for the medium term but not for the forward estimates, and that decade-long projections are unreliable.

Original statement

“You just made those numbers up ... You literally made those numbers up.”

9 April 2025 · Sky News Treasurers' Debate, responding to Angus Taylor's statement that groceries had risen 30 per cent and gas, electricity and insurance by more than 30 per cent under Labor

Original source

  • Tier 2 · IndependentTranscript· 9 April 2025
    The Treasurers' Debate: Jim Chalmers v Angus Taylor (video, from 29:05)skynews.com.au

    Sky News Australia

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

ABS monthly consumer price data show gas and other household fuels rose 34 per cent between May 2022 and February 2025, and quarterly data show insurance rose 35 per cent between June 2022 and December 2024. AAP FactCheck replicated the Coalition's electricity figure of about 32 per cent on the ABS index that excludes government rebates, although electricity prices including rebates rose only about 1 per cent. Food and non-alcoholic beverages rose 13 per cent, well below the 30 per cent grocery figure, which Mr Taylor's office sourced from an unpublished News Corp supermarket basket analysis. Three of the four figures the Treasurer said were made up were supported by official data on the measures the Coalition used.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    Consumer Price Index, Australia, December quarter 2024abs.gov.au

    Australian Bureau of Statistics — Insurance prices up 35 per cent between June 2022 and December 2024.

Original statement

“The biggest risk to Australians on low and fixed incomes is Peter Dutton ... He describes the indexation of pensions and payments as wasteful spending.”

11 March 2025 · Joint media release with the Minister for Social Services

Original source

  • Tier 3 · SupportingOther· 11 March 2025
    Joint media release, Treasurer and Minister for Social Services, 11 March 2025 (archived copy)archive.md

    Treasurer of Australia (archived by AAP FactCheck)

Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

The evidence the ministers' offices supplied to AAP FactCheck, an ABC Insiders interview with the Opposition Leader in February 2025, an interview with the Shadow Treasurer in May 2024 and the Opposition Leader's 2023 budget reply, shows the phrase 'wasteful spending' was used about federal public service numbers and the NDIS, not about indexation. The Shadow Treasurer's $45 billion figure for spending the Coalition had opposed excluded social security payments. AAP FactCheck found no record of the Opposition Leader describing indexation as wasteful, and the opposition confirmed that cutting indexation was not on its agenda. Social policy experts said the transcripts did not support the claim.
