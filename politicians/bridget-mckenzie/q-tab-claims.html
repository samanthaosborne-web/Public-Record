Original statement

“Labor's family car tax will put thousands of dollars on the price of new cars Australians love to drive”

19 February 2024 · Joint media release with Ted O'Brien, published on the Liberal Party website

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryMedia release· 19 February 2024
    Joint media release: Labor's family car tax will put thousands of dollars on the price of new cars Australians love to driveliberal.org.au

    Liberal Party of Australia (Bridget McKenzie and Ted O'Brien)

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

Economists Paul Burke (ANU) and John Quiggin (University of Queensland) and tax law professor Michael Dirkis (University of Sydney) told RMIT ABC Fact Check that the New Vehicle Efficiency Standard is not a tax because it raises no government revenue: it sets fleet-wide emissions limits on manufacturers, with tradeable credits and penalties for non-compliance. The Federal Chamber of Automotive Industries, whose modelling was cited for specific price rises, said its modelling of penalties and credits 'is not a forecast on price impacts'. The fact-check concluded that 'the government's proposal does not amount to a new tax' and that claims of specific price rises were not supported by the industry modelling cited.

Contradictory evidence

The primary evidence listed above supplies the context the claim needs; no separate contradicting source is attached.

Context

The fact-check's headline subject was Opposition Leader Peter Dutton; the McKenzie and O'Brien release was cited as one example of the same claim, alongside statements by Angus Taylor and Michaelia Cash. PUBLIC RECORD classifies the claim as mixed: the 'tax' characterisation was found not to apply, and the price-impact figure was not supported by the modelling cited, while the policy does impose penalties on manufacturers that could affect prices. No response from Senator McKenzie is recorded in the fact-check.
