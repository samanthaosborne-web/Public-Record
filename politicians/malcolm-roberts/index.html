Parliamentary career

Elected to the Senate for Queensland at the 2016 election. The Parliament of Australia records that this election was ruled void on 27 October 2017 under section 44(i) of the Constitution, with ineligibility dating from 2 July 2016. Elected to the Senate for Queensland again at the 2019 election, with service from 1 July 2019, and re-elected in 2025. Recorded as a member of Pauline Hanson's One Nation until 28 January 2026 and of One Nation from that date.

Original statement

“Scientific records show that bleaching has been a natural part of the Great Barrier Reef's cycles and other reef cycles for millennia ... That is fact.”

March 2024 · Senate speech on a motion about the Great Barrier Reef (March 2024; published on the senator's website and as a video on X on 25 March 2024)

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryTranscript
    The Great Barrier Reef is not dying (speech text and video)malcolmrobertsqld.com.au

    Senator Malcolm Roberts (website)

Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

Australian Institute of Marine Science records show mass bleaching on the reef was first documented with full-scale surveys in 1998, followed by 2002, 2016, 2017, 2020 and 2022, with the 2024 event the fifth in eight years. A University of Queensland coral scientist told AAP FactCheck that cores from corals hundreds of years old show no earlier mass bleaching events, and a co-author of the University of Sydney study cited in the speech said it examined reef growth over 20,000 to 30,000-year cycles and did not look at bleaching.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report
    Coral bleaching events on the Great Barrier Reefaims.gov.au

    Australian Institute of Marine Science — Mass bleaching documented with full-scale surveys in 1998, 2002, 2016, 2017, 2020 and 2022.

  • Tier 3 · SupportingOther
    University of Sydney reef-growth study cited in the speechsciencedirect.com

    University of Sydney via ScienceDirect — Per its co-author, the study assesses reef growth over 20,000 to 30,000-year cycles and does not examine bleaching.

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryTranscript· 7 February 2024

Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

The National Greenhouse Gas Inventory quarterly update to September 2023 attributes 17.9 per cent of Australia's emissions to agriculture, while the land use, land-use change and forestry sector offset 13.9 per cent of total emissions. Scientists from the CSIRO and the Global Carbon Project told AAP FactCheck that no single sector can count natural sinks as cancelling its own emissions.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report

    Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water — Agriculture 17.9 per cent of national emissions in the year to September 2023; land sector offset 13.9 per cent.

  • Tier 3 · SupportingOther
    Methane emissions factsheetunep.org

    United Nations Environment Programme — About 25 per cent of current warming comes from methane.

Original statement

“...And this doesn't include the 2.7 million visa holders in Australia...”

30 January 2024 · Post on X, sharing an ABC News post about the population reaching 27 million

Original source

  • Tier 3 · SupportingOther· 30 January 2024
    Post on X, 30 January 2024twitter.com

    Malcolm Roberts (X)

Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

The ABS told AAP FactCheck that its estimated resident population, which underlies the population clock that passed 27 million on 24 January 2024, counts anyone in Australia for 12 of 16 months regardless of visa type. Most of the 2.76 million temporary visa holders recorded by the Department of Home Affairs at 31 December 2023, including 701,868 New Zealanders on Special Category visas and 547,075 student visa holders, are therefore already included; only the 601,051 visitor-visa holders would mostly be excluded.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    Population clock and pyramidabs.gov.au

    Australian Bureau of Statistics — The clock projects the estimated resident population; it passed 27 million on 24 January 2024.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    Technical note: the 12/16 month rule for measuring overseas migrationabs.gov.au

    Australian Bureau of Statistics — A person is counted in the resident population if in Australia for 12 months or more over a 16-month period, irrespective of visa.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    Temporary entrants visa holders dataset, 31 December 2023data.gov.au

    Department of Home Affairs via data.gov.au — 2.76 million temporary visa holders, including 701,868 Special Category, 547,075 student and 601,051 visitor visa holders.

Context
