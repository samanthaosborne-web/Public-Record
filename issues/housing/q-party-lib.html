Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

NAB's 17 August 2026 economic newsletter described an illustrative scenario: if rental yields rose by one percentage point and property prices stayed flat, Sydney and Melbourne rents would need to rise by 25 to 30 per cent. It said any adjustment was more likely to combine lower dwelling prices and higher rents. In a note issued on 18 August, before the question was asked, NAB stated that the figure 'was not a forecast'. Ray White, whose modelling produced a similar figure, said its scenario assumed no price falls, which it considered unlikely, and was 'not a forecast of rent growth'. No published NAB material forecast a 30 per cent rise in rents.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report

    Australian Taxation Office — The May 2026 budget changes to negative gearing and capital gains tax under debate.

    National Australia Bank — Illustrative scenario in which rents would need to rise 25 to 30 per cent if yields rose one point and prices were unchanged; says the adjustment is more likely a mix of higher rents and lower values.

  • Tier 3 · SupportingOther

    Ray White Group — Models a rise of about 30 per cent only in a no-price-fall scenario that it describes as unlikely.

Original statement

“Around half a million people coming to the country per year has put pressure on our services, pressure on our health system, pressure on our infrastructure, on our roads, and certainly pressure on housing as well.”

15 February 2026 · Interview, ABC Insiders

Original source

  • Tier 2 · IndependentTranscript· 15 February 2026

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

ABS overseas migration data show net overseas migration peaked at 555,800 in the year to September 2023, then fell to 364,820 a year later and to 305,570 in the year to June 2025. ANU demographer Alan Gamlen put the five-year average at about 260,000 a year. Gross arrivals were 568,370 in the year to June 2025, below the 607,870 recorded in the year to December 2019. The 'half a million' figure applied to one post-pandemic year, not to migration 'per year' at the time of the statement.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    Overseas Migration (latest release)abs.gov.au

    Australian Bureau of Statistics — Net overseas migration of 555,800 in 2022-23 falling to 305,570 in the year to June 2025; arrivals and departures series.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report
    Visa application fee waivers for students and working holiday makers (2022)immi.homeaffairs.gov.au

    Department of Home Affairs — Coalition-era policy that lifted arrivals after the borders reopened.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report
    COVID-19 Pandemic event visa (subclass 408)immi.homeaffairs.gov.au

    Department of Home Affairs — Visa that allowed temporary entrants to extend their stays, reducing departures.
