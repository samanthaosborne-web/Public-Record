Original statement

“They're going to bring in 1.8 million new people into this country over the next five years with nowhere to put them”

March 2025 · Interview, 4BC Breakfast radio, Brisbane (March 2025; exact date not stated in the fact-check)

Original source

  • Tier 3 · SupportingOther
    4BC Breakfast interview (audio)omny.fm

    4BC (Omny)

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

Mr Littleproud's and Angus Taylor's offices said the figure summed ABS net overseas migration for 2022-23 and 2023-24 with budget forecasts for 2024-25 to 2026-27, giving 1.791 million; 54 per cent of that total comes from the two post-pandemic years already past. Summing the forecasts for the next five years, as the wording 'over the next five years' implies, gives about 1.27 million, because net migration is forecast to fall sharply from 2024-25. Migration experts Anna Boucher, Alan Gamlen and Abul Rizvi told AAP FactCheck that the post-pandemic surge largely reflected reduced departures and the policies of both Coalition and Labor governments, and that net migration should not be equated with arrivals.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    National, state and territory population, December 2025abs.gov.au

    Australian Bureau of Statistics — Actual net overseas migration for 2022-23 and 2023-24.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report
    Budget 2025-26, Budget Paper No. 1budget.gov.au

    Australian Government (Treasury) — Net overseas migration forecasts for 2024-25 to 2026-27, falling sharply; forward five-year sum of about 1.27 million.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report
    COVID-19 Pandemic event visa (subclass 408)immi.homeaffairs.gov.au

    Department of Home Affairs — Coalition-era visa that allowed temporary entrants to extend their stays, reducing departures.

Independent evidence

  • Tier 2 · IndependentFact check· 28 March 2025
    Experts: Coalition migration claim omits key contextaap.com.au

    AAP FactCheck — Verdict as published: No verdict label; AAP FactCheck found the claim 'omits key context'.

Contradictory evidence

The primary evidence listed above supplies the context the claim needs; no separate contradicting source is attached.

Context

The same figure was used by Angus Taylor and Peter Dutton in March 2025 and is recorded separately for Mr Taylor. The 1.8 million figure is the sum for a five-year period that includes two years already elapsed; applied to the next five years, as stated, it is overstated by about half a million.

Politician response

  • 28 March 2025 · defence

    Mr Littleproud's and Mr Taylor's offices told AAP FactCheck the claim was based on net overseas migration data over the five years from 2022-23 to 2026-27.

Original statement

“This government now has a seven-star construction code adding $60,000 to the construction of a home”

17 February 2025 · Interview, Sky News Australia

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryTranscript· 17 February 2025
    Transcript: interview, Sky News Australia, 17 February 2025 (ParlInfo)parlinfo.aph.gov.au

    Parliament of Australia (ParlInfo)

Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

The offices of Mr Littleproud and Michael Sukkar said the figure came from Master Builders Australia and supplied a table comparing ABS average values of new home building approvals in Greater Sydney between the December quarters of 2022 and 2023. That comparison measured overall cost increases from labour, materials and regulation, not the cost of the 2022 National Construction Code seven-star change, as Master Builders itself confirmed. Other estimates of the code change were $2,972 to $5,905 (Australian Glass and Window Association), $20,000 to $30,000 (Master Builders Queensland), $25,000 (Housing Industry Association) and $464 to $7,830 in capital costs with $630 to $9,693 in energy-bill savings (ACIL Allen regulatory impact statement). Building researchers Trivess Moore (RMIT) and Michael Ambrose (CSIRO) said no rigorous study supported $60,000.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report

    Australian Building Codes Board — What the code is and the 2022 seven-star energy update.

  • Tier 2 · IndependentOfficial report

    ACIL Allen for the Australian Building Codes Board — Capital costs of $464 to $7,830 and bill savings of $630 to $9,693 depending on house type and location.

  • Tier 3 · SupportingOther
    Builders fighting to keep the Queensland building dream alivembqld.com.au

    Master Builders Queensland — Industry estimate of $20,000 to $30,000 compliance cost.

  • Tier 3 · SupportingOther
    Interview with Laura Jayes, Sky Newsmasterbuilders.com.au

    Master Builders Australia — Master Builders' chief executive attributing cost rises to labour shortages, materials and regulation combined.

Independent evidence

    AAP FactCheck — Verdict as published: False.

Contradictory evidence

The primary evidence listed above is inconsistent with the claim as stated; no separate contradicting source is attached.

Context

The same claim was made by Peter Dutton in October 2024 and by Michael Sukkar on 2CC radio. Industry estimates of the code change vary widely, but none located by AAP FactCheck approached $60,000. Mr Littleproud's office did not issue a correction.

Politician response

  • 5 March 2025 · defence

    Mr Littleproud's and Mr Sukkar's offices told AAP FactCheck the figure came from Master Builders Australia and provided a table of calculations based on ABS data.
