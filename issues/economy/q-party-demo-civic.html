“Household disposable income has fallen 8 per cent since this government was elected.”

Marcus Oyelaran·Civic Reform·3 February 2026·Doorstop interview, Parliament House

What the evidence shows

The national accounts show real household disposable income per capita fell 4.6 per cent between the June quarter 2022 and the December quarter 2025. Nominal household disposable income rose 11 per cent over the same period. Neither series shows an 8 per cent fall.

Context

'Household disposable income' can be measured in nominal or real terms, in aggregate or per person. The per-capita real measure fell; the aggregate nominal measure rose. Mr Oyelaran subsequently corrected the figure to 'around 5 per cent in real per-capita terms'.
