Original statement

“Forty per cent of the nation's food comes out of Farrer, so it's pretty important.”

23 August 2026 · Interview, ABC TV Insiders

Original source

  • Tier 2 · IndependentTranscript· 23 August 2026
    Insiders, 23 August 2026 (video, from 27:45)youtu.be

    ABC News (YouTube)

Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

Using ABS 2020–21 value-of-production data for the 48 statistical areas wholly or partly within Farrer, an analysis by Flinders University water economist Professor Sarah Wheeler for AAP FactCheck estimated the electorate's agricultural output at about $4.4 billion of a $71 billion national total, around 6 per cent. The 40 per cent figure matches the whole Murray-Darling Basin, which the Murray-Darling Basin Authority says produces around 40 per cent of Australia's agricultural output and which is roughly eight times the size of the electorate.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    Value of Agricultural Commodities Produced, Australia, 2020-21abs.gov.au

    Australian Bureau of Statistics — Gross value of agricultural production by region; national total about $71 billion; Murray-Darling Basin about $30 billion.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report
    The Basinmdba.gov.au

    Murray-Darling Basin Authority — Around 40 per cent of Australia's agricultural produce comes from the Basin.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial statement
    Profile of the electoral division of Farrer (NSW)aec.gov.au

    Australian Electoral Commission — Farrer covers 126,563 square kilometres, about one-eighth of the Basin.

Independent evidence

  • Tier 2 · IndependentFact check· 28 August 2026

Contradictory evidence

Context

In a Facebook video on 28 June 2026 Mr Farley applied the 40 per cent figure to the Southern Murray-Darling Basin, an area about four times larger than Farrer.

Original statement

“The Albanese Labor Government's poor management of immigration, energy policy, and the economy has left Australians suffering through the worst per-capita recession in our nation's history.”

December 2024 · Post on Facebook (December 2024; exact date not stated in the fact-check)

Original source

  • Tier 3 · SupportingOther
    Archived copy of the Facebook postmvau.lt

    Pauline Hanson (Facebook), archived by AAP FactCheck

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

ABS national accounts to the September quarter 2024 recorded seven consecutive quarterly falls in GDP per capita, the longest run in the series that begins in 1973, with a cumulative fall of about 2.1 per cent since December 2022. Measured by depth, larger per-capita falls occurred in the June quarter 2020 (6.9 per cent), in 1981–83 (6.2 per cent) and in 1990–91 (3 per cent), and economic historians cited falls of around 20 per cent in 1929–31 and 17 per cent in the 1890s. The claim holds for the number of consecutive quarters but not for the size of the fall.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    Australian National Accounts: National Income, Expenditure and Product, September 2024abs.gov.au

    Australian Bureau of Statistics — GDP per capita fell 0.3 per cent in the quarter and 1.5 per cent over the year: seven consecutive quarterly declines.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    Labour Force, Australia, November 2024abs.gov.au

    Australian Bureau of Statistics — Unemployment of 4 per cent in November 2024, against peaks of 11.1 per cent in 1992 and 10.4 per cent in 1983.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report
    Research Discussion Paper 2001-07: The 1890s depressionrba.gov.au

    Reserve Bank of Australia — Real GDP fell about 17 per cent in the 1890s depression.

Independent evidence

  • Tier 2 · IndependentFact check· 8 January 2025
    Pauline Hanson's 'worst' per capita recession claim is mostly falseaap.com.au

    AAP FactCheck — Verdict as published: Mostly false.

Contradictory evidence

The primary evidence listed above supplies the context the claim needs; no separate contradicting source is attached.

Context

'Worst' can be measured by duration or by depth. Senator Hanson's spokesperson told AAP FactCheck she was referring to the number of consecutive quarters of negative per-capita growth. AAP FactCheck rated the claim 'mostly false'; PUBLIC RECORD classifies it as mixed because it is accurate on one measure and inaccurate on the other.

Politician response

  • 8 January 2025 · clarification

    A spokesperson told AAP FactCheck that Senator Hanson was referring to the number of consecutive quarters of negative per-capita GDP growth, citing a news.com.au graph and an Australian Institute of Company Directors article describing the run as the longest on record.

  • ClarifiedThrough a spokesperson, Senator Hanson clarified that the statement referred to the number of consecutive quarters of falling per-capita GDP rather than the depth of the fall. The original post was not amended as far as PUBLIC RECORD has located.
