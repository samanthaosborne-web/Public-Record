Original statement

“The last time government changed in Australia, in 2013, in the 2014 budget $30 billion was ripped out of public schools in that budget”

April 2025 · Televised leaders' debates during the 2025 election campaign (Sky News and ABC debates; exact dates not stated in the fact-check)

Original source

  • Tier 2 · IndependentTranscript
    Sky News leaders' debate, April 2025 (video, from 1:23)youtu.be

    Sky News Australia (YouTube)

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

The figure traces to a 2014-15 Treasury estimate, given in answer to a Senate estimates question, that replacing Labor's school funding indexation with indexation to inflation plus enrolment growth would reduce projected Commonwealth spending by about $28 billion between 2017-18 and 2024-25. That was a reduction in the projected rate of future increases, not a cut to existing funding. The 2014 budget committed to the four funded years of Labor's 2013 plan; Labor's six-year plan had only about a third of its cost budgeted and four jurisdictions signed up. Public finance experts consulted by AAP FactCheck described the change as a re-estimation of unfunded commitments rather than a cut.

    Australian Government (Treasury) — $2.8 billion allocated to additional schools funding over the first four years of Labor's plan.

Context

The Labor campaign referred AAP FactCheck to page seven of the 2014 budget overview as evidence. The Prime Minister made the claim several times during the 2025 campaign, including in both the Sky News and ABC leaders' debates; the Education Minister used the same figure. The dispute is over whether a reduction in projected future growth should be described as money 'ripped out'.
