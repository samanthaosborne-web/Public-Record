Original statement

“One of the things that my government has done, of course, is introduce energy bill relief - three lots of energy bill relief, all of which were opposed by those opposite”

31 July 2025 · House of Representatives, Question Time, responding to a question from the Leader of the Opposition

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryHansard· 31 July 2025
    House of Representatives Hansard, 31 July 2025aph.gov.au

    Parliament of Australia

Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

Parliamentary records show the Coalition voted against the December 2022 legislation that created the Energy Bill Relief Fund and funded the first round, although the then Opposition Leader said it would have supported the subsidies if they had been separated from the coal and gas price caps in the same bill. The $300 extension in the 2024-25 budget passed in an appropriation bill without Coalition opposition after the Opposition Leader confirmed support in his budget reply of 17 May 2024. The third round, announced in the 2025-26 budget, lapsed at the dissolution of Parliament, was reintroduced in July 2025, and the then Shadow Treasurer had said in March 2025 the Coalition would not oppose it. AAP FactCheck found no evidence the Coalition opposed the second or third rounds.

Original statement

“You just made those numbers up ... You literally made those numbers up.”

9 April 2025 · Sky News Treasurers' Debate, responding to Angus Taylor's statement that groceries had risen 30 per cent and gas, electricity and insurance by more than 30 per cent under Labor

Original source

  • Tier 2 · IndependentTranscript· 9 April 2025
    The Treasurers' Debate: Jim Chalmers v Angus Taylor (video, from 29:05)skynews.com.au

    Sky News Australia

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

ABS monthly consumer price data show gas and other household fuels rose 34 per cent between May 2022 and February 2025, and quarterly data show insurance rose 35 per cent between June 2022 and December 2024. AAP FactCheck replicated the Coalition's electricity figure of about 32 per cent on the ABS index that excludes government rebates, although electricity prices including rebates rose only about 1 per cent. Food and non-alcoholic beverages rose 13 per cent, well below the 30 per cent grocery figure, which Mr Taylor's office sourced from an unpublished News Corp supermarket basket analysis. Three of the four figures the Treasurer said were made up were supported by official data on the measures the Coalition used.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    Monthly Consumer Price Index Indicator, February 2025abs.gov.au

    Australian Bureau of Statistics — Gas and other household fuels up 34 per cent and food up 13 per cent since May 2022; electricity up about 1 per cent including rebates.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryDataset
    Consumer Price Index, Australia, December quarter 2024abs.gov.au

    Australian Bureau of Statistics — Insurance prices up 35 per cent between June 2022 and December 2024.

Independent evidence

  • Tier 2 · IndependentFact check· 17 April 2025
    Chalmers' 'made up' inflation stats claim misleadsaap.com.au

    AAP FactCheck — Verdict as published: Misleading.

Contradictory evidence

The primary evidence listed above supplies the context the claim needs; no separate contradicting source is attached.

Context

The exchange occurred during the 2025 election campaign. The grocery figure was not supported by ABS data, but the gas, insurance and ex-rebate electricity figures were. The Treasurer's office did not respond to AAP FactCheck's request for evidence that the numbers were made up.
