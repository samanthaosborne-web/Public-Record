Original statement

“It allows the government to ban people from certain countries from ever travelling to Australia. The government has not said what countries this will apply to. But it could be literally any country.”

15 April 2024 · Video posted on X describing the Migration Amendment (Removal and Other Measures) Bill 2024 as a 'Trump-style travel ban bill'

Evidence status

Mostly supportedThe evidence supports the substance of the claim with minor inaccuracies or omissions.

The bill would allow the immigration minister to designate any country a 'removal concern country' if the minister thought it in the national interest, after which that country's citizens outside Australia could not make valid visa applications, with exemptions for dual nationals, the partners, children and parents of Australians, and refugee and humanitarian applicants. The explanatory memorandum describes the designation as a 'discretionary personal power'. Legal experts from the Kaldor Centre, the University of Sydney, the University of Technology Sydney and the University of Queensland, and the Law Council of Australia, told RMIT ABC Fact Check that the discretion was exceptionally broad with very limited scope for judicial review, and broader than the United States visa-sanction power that the Trump administration applied to nine countries between 2017 and 2020, which mostly targeted government officials. One expert noted that a court could strike down an unreasonable designation, so 'literally any country' is not certain in practice.

Contradictory evidence

Context

RMIT ABC Fact Check found the claim 'a fair call'. The bill was introduced after the High Court's NZYQ decision ended indefinite immigration detention; the government described the power as a diplomatic tool to encourage countries to accept the return of their nationals. A spokesman for Senator Shoebridge told Fact Check that while there were 'some minor differences' from the Trump-era orders, 'the purpose of the bill is the same'.

Politician response

  • 6 June 2024 · defence

    A spokesman for Senator Shoebridge told RMIT ABC Fact Check that the bill would let the government prevent people from selected countries applying for Australian visas, 'effectively creating a travel ban', that the comparison was with the Trump executive orders restricting visa applications from selected countries, and that numerous legal experts had drawn the same comparison in Senate inquiry submissions.

    “While there are some minor differences, the purpose of the bill is the same.”
