“BREAKING: The CSIRO has finally admitted that coal is the cheapest form of electricity. And this is even after they bias almost all of their assumptions against coal and in favour of wind and solar!”

28 July 2025 · Post on Facebook, featuring a cropped image of the CSIRO GenCost levelised-cost-of-electricity chart

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryTranscript· 28 July 2025
    Facebook post, 28 July 2025facebook.com

    Matt Canavan (Facebook)

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

The CSIRO's GenCost 2024-25 final report gives a 2024 levelised cost range of $111 to $178 per megawatt hour for black coal and $120 to $168 for solar and wind with firming, with near-identical midpoints ($144.50 and $144). For 2030 the ranges are $103 to $174 for coal (midpoint $138.50) and $76 to $116 for firmed renewables (midpoint $96). The post used only the low end of the 2024 coal range and omitted the 2030 part of the chart. GenCost's lead author and a CSIRO spokesperson told AAP FactCheck that midpoints are the reasonable comparison and that renewables remain the lowest-cost new-build generation technology.

“The most popular selling vehicle in Australia - the Ford Everest - will go up by $6000 in purchase price this year, going up to $18,000 as it phases in. And then a RAV4 ... that's going up by nearly $3000 and going up by over $11,000 over this phase-in period.”

1 January 2025 · Interview, Sky News Australia

Original source

  • Tier 2 · IndependentNews report· 1 January 2025
    David Littleproud warns Aussies they will pay more for cars as Labor's New Vehicle Efficiency Standard comes into effectskynews.com.au

    Sky News Australia

Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

Mr Littleproud's office said the figures came from Federal Chamber of Automotive Industries modelling from February 2024, which used a stricter draft of the policy, 2023 sales data, the Ford Ranger as a proxy for the Everest, and petrol-only RAV4 models that Toyota stopped selling in June 2024; the chamber itself described the figures as 'outdated somewhat'. Applying the legislated formula, AAP FactCheck calculated that every 2025 RAV4 model would earn a credit of $3,390 to $3,880 (confirmed by Toyota), that the highest-emitting Everest would incur a penalty of $3,210 (an average of $420 across nine models), and that by 2029 RAV4 penalties would be $4,420 to $4,910 rather than $11,000. The Ford Everest was not Australia's most popular vehicle in 2024. Transport researchers Robin Smit (UTS) and Hussein Dia (Swinburne) said fleet-wide penalties do not translate into fixed price rises for particular models.

Original statement

“Labor's family car tax will put thousands of dollars on the price of new cars Australians love to drive”

19 February 2024 · Joint media release with Ted O'Brien, published on the Liberal Party website

Original source

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryMedia release· 19 February 2024
    Joint media release: Labor's family car tax will put thousands of dollars on the price of new cars Australians love to driveliberal.org.au

    Liberal Party of Australia (Bridget McKenzie and Ted O'Brien)

Evidence status

Mixed / context requiredThe claim is accurate under some definitions, periods or measures but not others. The context matters.

Economists Paul Burke (ANU) and John Quiggin (University of Queensland) and tax law professor Michael Dirkis (University of Sydney) told RMIT ABC Fact Check that the New Vehicle Efficiency Standard is not a tax because it raises no government revenue: it sets fleet-wide emissions limits on manufacturers, with tradeable credits and penalties for non-compliance. The Federal Chamber of Automotive Industries, whose modelling was cited for specific price rises, said its modelling of penalties and credits 'is not a forecast on price impacts'. The fact-check concluded that 'the government's proposal does not amount to a new tax' and that claims of specific price rises were not supported by the industry modelling cited.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report
    Cleaner, cheaper to run cars: the Australian New Vehicle Efficiency Standard, consultation impact analysis (February 2024)infrastructure.gov.au

    Department of Infrastructure, Transport, Regional Development, Communications and the Arts — The policy options, fleet-wide targets, credits and penalties.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryMedia release
    Joint media release: Delivering Australians more choice of cars that save on fuelminister.dcceew.gov.au

    Minister for Climate Change and Energy — The government's preferred model for the standard.

Independent evidence

  • Tier 2 · IndependentNews report· 15 March 2024
    Peter Dutton says Labor's proposed fuel efficiency standards constitute a ute or family car tax. Is that correct?abc.net.au

    RMIT ABC Fact Check (CheckMate newsletter) — Verdict as published: No verdict label (CheckMate newsletter); finding headed 'No, Labor is not taxing the family car'.

Contradictory evidence

The primary evidence listed above supplies the context the claim needs; no separate contradicting source is attached.

Context

The fact-check's headline subject was Opposition Leader Peter Dutton; the McKenzie and O'Brien release was cited as one example of the same claim, alongside statements by Angus Taylor and Michaelia Cash. PUBLIC RECORD classifies the claim as mixed: the 'tax' characterisation was found not to apply, and the price-impact figure was not supported by the modelling cited, while the policy does impose penalties on manufacturers that could affect prices. No response from Senator McKenzie is recorded in the fact-check.
