Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

Using ABS 2020–21 value-of-production data for the 48 statistical areas wholly or partly within Farrer, an analysis by Flinders University water economist Professor Sarah Wheeler for AAP FactCheck estimated the electorate's agricultural output at about $4.4 billion of a $71 billion national total, around 6 per cent. The 40 per cent figure matches the whole Murray-Darling Basin, which the Murray-Darling Basin Authority says produces around 40 per cent of Australia's agricultural output and which is roughly eight times the size of the electorate.

    Australian Electoral Commission — Farrer covers 126,563 square kilometres, about one-eighth of the Basin.

Original statement

“While the landholders that might get the turbines and solar panels, they may get some financial benefit, those with the transmission lines don't.”

18 August 2024 · Interview, ABC Insiders

Original source

  • Tier 2 · IndependentTranscript· 18 August 2024
    Insiders, 18 August 2024 (video, from 12:57)youtube.com

    ABC News (YouTube)

Evidence status

Contradicted by evidenceThe best available evidence is inconsistent with the claim as stated.

Landholders who host new transmission lines receive compensation for easements and, in several states, additional annual payments: New South Wales and Victoria pay about $200,000 per kilometre of line hosted over 20 to 25 years, and Queensland about $300,000 per kilometre. AAP FactCheck also cited the Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water and the Australian Energy Infrastructure Commissioner on easement compensation. The statement that transmission-line hosts receive no financial benefit was not correct.

Primary evidence

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report
    Strategic Benefit Payments Scheme: overviewenergyco.nsw.gov.au

    EnergyCo (NSW Government) — NSW scheme paying landholders about $200,000 per kilometre of transmission line hosted, in annual instalments over 20 years.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report
    Compensation and landholder payments factsheetenergy.vic.gov.au

    Victorian Government (Energy) — Victorian payments of about $200,000 per kilometre over 25 years.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report
    Transmission: community engagementdcceew.gov.au

    Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water — Landholders with an easement on their property are compensated by transmission companies, which must negotiate access.

  • Tier 1 · PrimaryOfficial report
    Observations and recommendations, chapter 1: host landowner negotiationsaeic.gov.au

    Australian Energy Infrastructure Commissioner — How host landholders are paid for wind, solar and transmission projects.

  • Tier 2 · IndependentOfficial report
    SuperGrid Landholder Payment Frameworkpowerlink.com.au

    Powerlink Queensland — Queensland framework with a baseline scenario of about $300,000 per kilometre, subject to land value.

Independent evidence

  • Tier 2 · IndependentFact check· 22 August 2024
    Nationals leader misses the mark on transmission lines claimaap.com.au

    AAP FactCheck — Verdict as published: Mostly False.

Contradictory evidence

The primary evidence listed above is inconsistent with the claim as stated; no separate contradicting source is attached.

Context

Asked for evidence, Mr Littleproud's office said he had misspoken. In a statement to AAP FactCheck he said: 'I acknowledge I misspoke in my answer in that interview. What I was trying to articulate was that those forced into hosting transmission lines don't receive an equitable level of financial benefit in comparison to the wind and solar hosts.' AAP FactCheck noted he had not otherwise publicly corrected the statement. AAP FactCheck's 'Mostly False' rating is recorded here as contradicted because the literal statement was shown to be incorrect; the clarified claim about the relative size of payments is a different claim that the fact-check did not assess.

Politician response

  • 22 August 2024 · clarification

    Mr Littleproud told AAP FactCheck he had misspoken and that he meant hosts of transmission lines do not receive an equitable level of financial benefit compared with wind and solar hosts.

    “I acknowledge I misspoke in my answer in that interview. What I was trying to articulate was that those forced into hosting transmission lines don't receive an equitable level of financial benefit in comparison to the wind and solar hosts.”
  • ClarifiedMr Littleproud acknowledged to AAP FactCheck that he misspoke and clarified what he meant. AAP FactCheck noted he had not otherwise publicly corrected the statement, and PUBLIC RECORD has located no public correction.
